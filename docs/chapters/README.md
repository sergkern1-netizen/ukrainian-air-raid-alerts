# Chapters

Each chapter is a self-contained analysis of one area of the alert record. Every chapter has a
short summary, its figures, a list of what it cannot tell you, and a method section. Every number
is reproduced by the chapter's script.

| # | Chapter | Question | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Timing and rhythms](01-timing-and-rhythms.md) | How long do alerts last, when do they start, and what does that mean for nights and routines? | Done |
| 2 | [Where sirens sound](02-regional-spread.md) | Which oblasts are most affected, how long do alerts last there, and how has the pattern changed? | Done |
| 3 | [Waves and clusters](03-waves-and-clusters.md) | How do alerts group into waves, how long do waves last, and which oblasts take part in them? | Done |
| 4 | [Feed reliability](04-feed-reliability.md) | How well do the volunteer and official records agree, by oblast and over time? | Done |
| 5 | [Long alerts](05-long-alerts.md) | What are the longest alerts, where are they, and are they real or recording errors? | Done |
| 6 | Granularity and the official feed | How did the official record change in December 2025, and what does that mean for comparisons? | Planned |
| 7 | Data provenance and quality | What is in each file, how were duplicates handled, and what is missing? | Planned |
| 8 | Reporting patterns | Why do the records disagree in the west, and what is the batching pattern in one oblast pair? | Planned |
| 9 | Daily and weekly disruption | How much of each day or week is interrupted, for schools, employers and logistics? | Planned |
| 10 | Method and reproducibility | How to rerun every chapter, and what each script reads and writes. | Planned |
